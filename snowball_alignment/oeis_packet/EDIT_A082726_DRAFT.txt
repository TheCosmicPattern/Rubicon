WORKING DRAFT of a proposed comment edit to A082726 (continued fraction of the
fine-structure constant). It is for the author to verify and word. The measurement values
tagged [recall] in ../checks/constants_as_bounds.py must first be confirmed against the
primary sources.

The problem with the current record:
  - "The term a(7) is today known to be in between 15 and 17, but most likely equal to 16."
  - "a(7) is believed to be 17 or 18 according to the revised 2014 CODATA value."
  Both comments state an uncertainty in words, without the rule that decides it. The
  second comment is exactly right for CODATA 2014 at one standard uncertainty (the
  script reproduces {17, 18}). Neither comment is current.

The exact rule (interval certification):
  A partial quotient is determined by a measurement only when every value in the stated
  interval has the same continued-fraction prefix through that term. At the first term
  where the interval crosses a boundary, every integer between the two endpoint
  expansions' terms is compatible and must be retained.

Proposed replacement comment (to be checked and reworded by the author):
%C A082726 The terms a(0)..a(6) are the same for every value of 1/alpha within one standard uncertainty of CODATA 2014, 2018 and 2022. a(7) is not determined: at one standard uncertainty, CODATA 2014 allows 17 or 18, CODATA 2018 allows 16 or 17, and CODATA 2022 allows 18 or 19. Of the individual determinations, the rubidium recoil measurement (Morel et al., 2020) gives a(7) = 19 and the electron g-2 determination (Fan et al., 2023) gives a(7) = 18, while the cesium recoil measurement (Parker et al., 2018) allows 15 or 16. The cesium and rubidium intervals do not overlap until each is widened to about 4.2 standard uncertainties.
