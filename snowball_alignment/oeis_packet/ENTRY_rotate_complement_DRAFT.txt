%N A?????? Number of cycles of the permutation of n-bit binary words that rotates a word cyclically one place and then complements every bit.
%C A?????? Writing the words as integers 0..2^n-1, the permutation is x -> (2^n-1) - rot(x). On 1..2^n-2 it is multiplication by -2 modulo 2^n-1. It exchanges 0 and 2^n-1, which are the two representatives of zero modulo 2^n-1.
%C A?????? Rotation and complementation commute. For odd n the permutation has order 2n and generates the whole group they generate, so its cycles are the necklaces counted by A000013. For even n it has order n; every odd power C*R^j fixes exactly 2^gcd(j,n) words, so the Burnside sum reduces to that of A000031.
%C A?????? In a normal basis of GF(2^n), where squaring is the cyclic shift and the all-ones word is 1, the permutation is z -> z^2 + 1 = (z+1)^2. Its n-th power is z -> z + 1 when n is odd.
%C A?????? For odd n there are no fixed points. For even n exactly two words are fixed, 0101...01 and 1010...10; in base 2^n these are the digits of 1/3 and 2/3 (for n=4, the hexadecimal digits 5 and A).
%C A?????? For a prime p = 2^n-1, the base-b expansions of k/p have this cycle structure on their remainders exactly when b == -2^j (mod p) with gcd(j,n)=1. For n=3 this includes b=10 (10 == -4 (mod 7)), so the 6-cycle is the remainder cycle 1,3,2,6,4,5 of 1/7 (read in reverse), whose digits are 142857 (A020806).
